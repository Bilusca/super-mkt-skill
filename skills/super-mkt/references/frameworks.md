# Frameworks, triggers and psychology

## Persuasion structures

Pick what the case calls for. Never force one.

| Structure | Sequence | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| AIDA | Attention · Interest · Desire · Action | Ads, email, short pages |
| PAS | Problem · Agitation · Solution | Conscious and latent pain |
| BAB / Before-After-Bridge | Before · After · Bridge | Visible transformation |
| QUEST | Qualify · Understand · Educate · Stimulate · Transition | Cold traffic, high ticket |
| 4Ps | Promise · Picture · Proof · Push | VSL, sales letter |
| ACCA | Awareness · Comprehension · Conviction · Action | Market unaware of the problem |
| StorySelling | A story that turns into the offer | High connection, low friction |

## Stages of awareness (Eugene Schwartz)

1. **Unaware** — doesn't know they have a problem. Open with a story or an unsettling fact, never with the product.
2. **Problem-aware** — feels the pain, doesn't know the solution. Lead with PAS.
3. **Solution-aware** — knows the categories. Lead with the unique mechanism.
4. **Product-aware** — knows you. Lead with proof and differentiators.
5. **Most aware** — only needs a reason to act now. Lead with the offer, guarantee, real scarcity.

Market sophistication dictates how much the promise must be reframed rather than amplified.

## Mental triggers

Use only when they fit. Never stack them.

Authority · Reciprocity · Scarcity · Urgency · Social proof · Exclusivity · Anticipation · Specificity · Novelty · Curiosity · Belonging · Safety · Consistency · Community · Transformation.

Scarcity and urgency only if **real**. Fake scarcity destroys credibility and long-term conversion.

## Applied biases and psychology

- **Loss aversion** — the cost of not acting hurts more than the gain of acting. Show the price of inaction.
- **Anchoring** — anchor value before price (cost of the problem, the expensive alternative, stacked bonus value).
- **Social proof** — a number, a name, a specific result. Real data only.
- **Confirmation bias** — open by agreeing with what the reader already believes.
- **Identity** — "this is for someone like you." Selling identity outconverts selling function.
- **Status** — what the purchase signals to others.
- **FOMO** — tied to a real event (cohort, batch, deadline).
- **Future pacing** — walk the reader through living the after.
- **Open loops** — raise a question in the hook, close it later. Sustains reading.
- **Commitment** — a micro-yes before the big yes.

## Storytelling

Structure: Context · Conflict · Discovery · Transformation · Lesson · Offer.

Rules: the protagonist resembles the reader; the conflict is specific, not abstract; the discovery is the unique mechanism; the lesson connects to the offer without feeling bolted on.
